What this page does not claim

Measured, mostly not yet heard

Every span here was placed by forced alignment against the audio and re-derived from the aligned word stream before printing. That is weaker than listening. Thirteen of these 41 talks were verified by ear in August; the rest have not been heard.

The dhyāna-ślokas are named by hand

No text in the corpus carries the invocations that open the discourses, so 32 of these recitations are named from a short list of dhyāna-ślokas kept by hand, at the maṭha’s request, and cited by their opening words rather than by a source work. Each is marked where it appears. Their wording is as it was checked against the recordings, and awaits the maṭha’s confirmation.

The closing nāmaghoṣa is named, and counted apart

«हर नमः पार्वतीपतये हर हर महादेव» closes 23 of these talks. It is an acclamation, not a verse quoted from a text, so it is named from the same hand-kept list and shown where it is chanted, but it is not counted among the recitations of verses, nor among what could not be named. Search hara to see every one.

Two talks whose counts answer a different question

Greatness of Sanskrit is delivered in Sanskrit throughout, so the script gate makes candidates of the Jagadguru's own prose — its 21 unnamed stretches are not missing texts, and they are left out of every count of what is not yet named. Misconceptions Dispelled is in Kannada and only its first four minutes carry Sanskrit in Devanagari; the rest was never searched.

Withheld

Anugraha Bhashanam | Laksharchana and kalashabhisheka to Lord Satyanarayana at Malla, Kerala ran, and is held back from this page because the classification is not to be trusted. Withholding is not deleting: the measurement stands on disk and is named here.

Where a citation is the wrong verse

A verse chanted from a text the index does not hold can still match a shared pāda somewhere else, and it does so at full confidence. The full note lists the cases found. Read a citation as the index's best address for what was chanted, not as a scholar's attribution.
