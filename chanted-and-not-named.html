Why this list is worth a text

Nine of the first ten are invocations — the ślokas to Śāradā, Sarasvatī and Śaṅkara that open the discourses. Each is chanted at every timestamp shown, and no text in the corpus can put a name to it. Digitizing one text on this list names it in every talk at once, including the talks not yet run.

Nothing here is named, and nothing here should be guessed at

Every passage was chanted and placed on the clock by measurement against the audio. None is addressed to any text, and none is guessed at: a machine’s guess under a Jagadguru’s chant would look exactly like an attribution and carry none of the weight of one. What is offered is the audio, the timestamp, and the characters as transcribed.

Those characters come from automatic transcription. A passage that reads as broken Sanskrit may be the transcription rather than the chanting — play the timestamp before concluding anything about it.

A timestamp is where the transcription starts

Each passage is shown as its fullest transcription. Where a recitation’s opening was not transcribed, its timestamp is the first word that was — so the chanting begins a little before it. There are 4 such onsets, marked part.
